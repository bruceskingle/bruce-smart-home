# GloBird

The GloBird module fetches a GloBird Energy customer's electricity data from GloBird's customer portal. It is new:

- **Meters**, the meters GloBird lists for the account, and the registers of the smart meter's readings, **E1** what was imported from the grid and **B1** what was exported to it. Click a register to see its readings every 5 minutes, by the tariff's charge types, e.g. Peak Usage and Offpeak Usage, as a graph or a table, as on Octopus's page of [a meter's readings](../octopus/meter.md). The graph's **Cost** shows what they cost, in AUD, worked out by the app, as GloBird gives only each day's costs: each reading's kWh at its charge type's rate, which the Days page works out from those costs, and E1's share of the day's supply charge, as GloBird charged it, every 5 minutes. A reading whose charge type has no rate, e.g. one too little used to tell, has no cost. B1's are the feed-in credits, below zero. A day's costs can differ by a cent or so from GloBird's, and more where GloBird charged some of a day's kWh on another day, or in summer, when the days here start an hour before GloBird's. Home's [Electricity](../electricity.md) page puts the import and export together. Times and days are Sydney's, by the clock. It starts at **5min**, each reading as GloBird gave it; at a longer period the readings are added up, so the table's kWh are in pink, as numbers the app works out are. **Reload** fetches the latest readings from GloBird.
- **Days**, each day's energy from the grid by the tariff's charge types, e.g. Peak Usage and Offpeak Usage, the energy exported, and the day's costs by category, in AUD. **Fetch** gets the days not cached yet, and the last few days again, as the meter's readings arrive a day or so late.
  - Numbers GloBird gave are in the usual colour; those the app works out, the rates, the number of readings and the total, are in pink.
  - **Rates**, above the days, are your tariff's rates in cents a kWh, and its daily charge, as GloBird charges them. GloBird's portal does not give them, so the app works them out from each day's costs and the meter's kWh. Each day's cost is rounded to the cent, so the rate of a charge type you use little, e.g. Peak Usage if batteries run the house then, is less exact, and one with too few kWh is not known. When the rates change, e.g. at GloBird's review each 1 July, a new set starts from that day.
  - Hold the pointer over a day's **USAGE** or **SOLAR** cost to see its split by charge type at those rates.
  - **Check** says when a day's charges do not fit the rates, and why if it can, e.g. that GloBird charged for fewer kWh than the meter measured that day, as it sometimes moves kWh from one day to the next. Such days are left out when the rates are worked out.
- **Bills**, the bills GloBird has emailed, read from their PDFs: each one's period and total, beside what the Days page's costs add up to over its period, in pink. Each **Fetch** makes one request to GloBird, the next which is needed, newest bill first, and says what the next will be, so that a long history is fetched a little at a time; press it again for more. Each bill is fetched once, as it never changes, and the list of emails again each day, to find new ones. Click a bill to see:
  - its charges as the bill says them, e.g. Peak Usage 5.83 kWh at 37.18c, beside the meter's kWh of that charge type over the bill's days, and the number of days, for the daily charge;
  - its charges added up by the categories of the days' costs, USAGE, SOLAR and SUPPLY, beside the days' costs of each, and the total;
  - **Open the PDF**, the bill itself, in a window of its own, which can sit beside the bill's page; **PDF** on the list of bills opens it too. The window is the app's own, listed in the Window menu, and Escape closes it. On Linux it opens in the system's PDF viewer instead.

  The days' figures are only all of the bill's when every one of its days is cached, which the page says. GloBird keeps only a few weeks of readings, so bills from before the module first fetched have no days to compare.
- **Cache Files**, every file it has fetched, as GloBird sent it.

Days run from midnight to midnight in Australian Eastern Standard Time (AEST, UTC+10) all year, without daylight saving, as GloBird's days and tariff times do, following the electricity market. So in summer, when the clocks in New South Wales, Victoria, the ACT and Tasmania go forward, a day starts at 1am by the clock there, and a peak of 3pm to 9pm is 4pm to 10pm by the clock.

GloBird keeps only a few weeks of readings, so the app keeps every day it fetches. Fetch at least every few weeks, or the days in between are lost.

[Logging in](login.md) explains what it needs to fetch your data.
